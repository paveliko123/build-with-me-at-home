Build With Me at Home

A parent and their kid build real apps together through the plan-first journey. It runs entirely on the family's computer, using the parent's own Claude Code login.

The kernel: a parent introducing their kid to Claude, with the parent kept in the loop.

Every finished app ends in two places. The kid gets a link to try it. The parent gets an Claude Opus report covering what was built, any red flags, and the link.

Who it's for

👩‍💻 The parent

Has a Claude subscription and Claude Code installed. "Likes coding or at least is not afraid of it." Installs once and owns the account.

🧒 Their kids

Under 13 and 13–17, guided by that parent. No adults as users, because the consumer terms don't allow sharing an account.

The core loop

parentkidsafety

Safety, in three layers

1
The guide knows who it's talking to

It's told it's helping a kid of a given age group, with age-appropriate rules. Claude's defaults are set for adults.

2
A live check on every turn

It checks what the kid says and what the guide replies, catching violent or graphic content and personal details.

3
The Opus parent report

The backstop: a separate review of the whole conversation, from the idea to the final app.

What "working" looks like

The parent signs in, and the kid talks through the journey and builds a real app. The app opens from its local link and stays on the My apps shelf. The "oh, that's cool" moment: the parent's report appears.

The boundary

NOW In the proof of concept
  • A local wrapper the parent starts with a double-click. Its own small server shows its screens and the kids' apps.
  • Runs on the parent's unmodified, already-signed-in Claude Code. The app never touches the sign-in or its tokens.
  • A parent sign-in gate with a plain-language "you're responsible" screen.
  • The kid and teen paths, with the existing design and flow, through all six steps.
  • Talking (speech in, voice out) without API keys.
  • The three safety layers above, all on the parent's Claude.
  • Each app as one HTML file in a folder, plus the My apps shelf and local links.
LATER Worth doing, not now
  • Sharing outside the home (hosting). Optional, to evaluate in step 5.
  • A more natural voice than the browser's own voices.
  • The "behind the scenes" learning session after an app is finished.
CUT Left behind, and why
  • Firestore and the Firebase setup: replaced by files on the family's computer.
  • API keys, App Check, and the Google Cloud voice: they need keys and accounts, and the goal is the simplest install.
  • Invite codes and the allowance: no per-app cost, so nothing to meter.
  • Adults as users: the consumer terms bar sharing an account.
  • Public-service compliance work: each install is a parent's own project. The conversation still reaches Anthropic under the parent's own account.

Why it matters

"Charging $2 per each app is not practical... My kid really loved the idea and was ready to build 5 apps a day."
"This becomes a personal project for each parent where everything stays local."

Learning goal: understand the pros, cons and limitations of running local copies.

Terms check (2026-10-06)

What the current Anthropic terms say

✓ Allowed: a user signing in to the unmodified Claude Code program with their own subscription, even when another product runs it (Claude Code legal page). Apps may not offer Claude login themselves or handle sign-in tokens.

✗ Constraint: the consumer accounts require users to be 18+, and "You also may not make your Account available to anyone else" (Consumer Terms).

Decision: the parent guides their own kids, with a parent gate and plain-language responsibility, and no sharing with other adults. Not legal advice.

Open for the PRD and Spec

  • What the parent gate actually is: the Claude sign-in on the machine, plus an app-level step such as a PIN.
  • Keeping "talking" local. Chrome's built-in speech recognition sends audio to Google.
  • How the wrapper runs Claude Code for the guide, builder, checks and report, and how much of the parent's subscription each app uses.
